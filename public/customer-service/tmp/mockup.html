One page for the whole journey

The Leads page and the Follow-up page track the same customers at different moments. This plan folds Follow-up into the Leads page you already use, so there is one list of things to do and one set of numbers, without throwing away either system's data.

What the two pages are today

Leads /customer-service/leads

"Did we call and quote the people who asked?"

  • One row per form submission (website form or Meta ad), combined when the same person submits twice within 7 days.
  • Calls are detected automatically from the phone system.
  • Quotes are attached automatically by matching email or phone against Shopify draft orders, once an hour.
  • Stages: New → Contacted → Quoted → Won / Lost / Not applicable.
  • Strong on speed: time to first call, time to first quote, split by source.

Follow-up /customer-service/follow-up

"Did we chase the quotes we sent?"

  • One row per Shopify draft order with an invoice sent, synced once a day plus a Live refresh.
  • Follow-ups are typed in by hand through the Log Follow-up form.
  • Stages: New → Hot lead / Considering / Price shopping / Future project / No answer → Won / Lost / Duplicate.
  • Won is detected automatically when the draft turns into an order.
  • Strong on cadence: due today, overdue, upcoming, attempts, loss reasons, timing settings.

What they share: the same customers, a "what's due" list, staff scoreboards, won and lost, duplicate handling, a store switcher, a Sync button, a Live toggle, and two separate cron jobs feeding them.

Where they don't line up, which is why a simple copy-paste merge would break:

  • Customers who walked in, phoned, or emailed exist only on Follow-up. Leads that were never quoted exist only on Leads. Neither list contains the other.
  • The link between a lead and its quote is a text match on email or phone, not a real key. A lead can only show one quote, even when it was re-quoted.
  • Won and lost are tracked twice. A quote marked Lost never marks the lead Lost, so the two conversion rates disagree.
  • "New" means "not called yet" on one page and "quoted, not chased yet" on the other.
  • Follow-up splits Toronto into two Shopify stores. Leads treats Toronto as one store.
  • The phone system already knows when staff call a quoted customer, but Follow-up ignores it and asks staff to log the call by hand.

The combined model

One row per customer. One ladder of stages that runs from the first contact to the close. The quote's follow-up status becomes the customer's stage the moment a quote goes out.

Combined page
  • A form submission creates the row, exactly as now. A quote sent to someone with no submission also creates a row, tagged "Quote only", so inquiry statistics keep ignoring it.
  • Calls count at every stage. Before the quote they mark the lead Contacted. After the quote they count as a follow-up attempt, and the queue asks the caller for the outcome instead of asking them to type the whole thing.
  • Won stays automatic: an order placed from the quote. Lost on the quote is Lost on the customer.
  • Duplicates are handled once, at the customer level: repeat submissions within 7 days and several open quotes for one email land in the same Duplicates filter.

Data changes, kept small

No table is deleted and no history is lost. Both pages keep working during the transition because they write to the same tables.

  • Add a lead reference to the quotes table. The matcher that already links quotes to leads simply saves the link it found. That turns a fuzzy text match into a real key and lets one customer own several quotes.
  • Create a customer row for quotes that match nobody, with source "quote".
  • Extend the lead's stage list with the follow-up statuses. The lead's own outcome field stops being edited on its own.
  • Copy Lost back to the customer when a quote is marked Lost. One line in the existing log action.
  • Record calls as follow-up attempts when the phone system sees a call to a quoted customer after the quote date.
  • Each migration is applied and verified on the live database before the code that depends on it ships. That order has caused outages before.

Why this is more efficient

  • One list in the morning. Calls to make and follow-ups due, sorted by urgency, instead of two pages checked separately.
  • Fewer things to type. Outbound calls to quoted customers are detected. Staff add the outcome, not the call.
  • One conversion rate instead of two that disagree.
  • One sync chain. Draft orders, quote links, and call links run in one scheduled sequence, which also makes the 300 second timeout easier to stay under.
  • One staff table for speed to call, quotes sent, follow-ups, and results.
  • Half the code to maintain. One dashboard, one API route, one drawer, one set of filters.

Rollout in four steps

Each step ships on its own. The Follow-up page stays live until the last one.

  1. Link the data

    Migration for the lead reference and the "quote only" source, then a backfill run of the existing matcher. Nothing visible changes.

  2. Extend the Leads page

    Add the To do, Upcoming, Addressed today, Open quotes, Closed, and Duplicates filters, the Next column, the Follow-up history section and outcomes in the drawer, and the reused Log Follow-up form. Staff can use either page meanwhile.

  3. Move the reporting, merge the staff table, switch on call detection

    Follow-up's charts and donuts go to Analysis. The two staff panels become one under the queue. Calls after the quote start counting as attempts.

  4. Retire the Follow-up page

    Its address redirects to the Leads To do filter. Remove the nav item and the WIP badge, point the home dashboard card and mobile screens at Leads, fold the two cron jobs together.

Decisions for you

  • NameKeep "Leads" for the combined page? "Pipeline" would clash with Revenue › Pipeline in the sidebar.
  • StoresKeep RF (both Toronto sites together) and BC, like Leads does today, with an optional filter by Shopify store on quotes? Or three separate stores, like Follow-up?
  • Awaiting quoteOwn filter, as drawn, or inside To do?
  • Quote-only customersFine to call them leads, or should they be labelled "Quotes" in the list?
  • Test ModeStill used for training? Keep or drop.
